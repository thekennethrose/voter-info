Every vote comes from the official roll call record and links to it: senate.gov for the Senate, the Clerk of the House for the House. Each bill is filed under the one policy area the Congressional Research Service assigns to it, so a bill that touches several issues appears under one; search finds the rest. Votes on amendments and procedural motions appear under the bill they concerned.

Not included yet: votes on treaties, quorum calls, and procedural votes not tied to a bill or nominee.

Terms like cloture, motion to proceed and suspension of the rules are explained in the glossary; How voting works covers the process.

About these statements and articles

On the floor lists what this member said or submitted in the Congressional Record, the official transcript of each chamber, published by the Government Publishing Office on GovInfo. The Record itself says who spoke. Senate statements the Record marks with a bullet as not spoken on the floor, and all Extensions of Remarks, are labeled as submitted. In the House section, the printed Record sets words inserted rather than spoken in a different typeface; the text version doesn't carry that, so House items are labeled as proceedings, not as speeches. Remarks under 50 words (mostly procedural requests) and purely procedural sections are left out.

In the media lists every article, in two independent news indexes, that names this member with their title or state, collected since the date shown above: GDELT (U.S. sources) and Media Cloud (its U.S. national and U.S. state and local collections). The indexes decide which outlets they cover; we don't choose outlets and don't rank articles. Headlines are each outlet's own. Listing an article is not an endorsement of it.

Same wording as the Record means the article contains at least 12 words in a row that match this member's words in the Record exactly (ignoring punctuation and capitalization). Usually that's a reporter quoting the member; it can also be wording the member's office used in both a statement and a press release. Phrases many members use, like standard procedural wording, don't count, and neither do runs that are mostly capitalized words, which are names such as a bill's title. We check only the text a reader can see: many sites send the whole article but hide part of it from non-subscribers, and wording found only in a hidden part doesn't count, since you couldn't confirm it by following the link. Articles we couldn't open weren't checked. A headline published by several sites, as wire stories are, is listed once with the other sites under it.

Outlet ratings will show how outside groups rate each outlet, attributed to them and never assigned by us, once we have permission to publish their ratings. None are shown yet.
